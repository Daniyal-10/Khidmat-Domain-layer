# 12. Significant Business Decisions

## 1. Partnership Authorization Decision
- **Purpose:** Legally and operationally committing to collaborate with another organisation.
- **Decision Maker:** Country Director / Head of Mission.
- **Supporting Evidence:** Due Diligence reports, capacity assessments, strategic alignment.
- **Governing Policies:** Organisational Partnership Frameworks, Anti-Terrorism Laws.
- **Constraints:** Heavy administrative burden of managing sub-grantees.
- **Preconditions:** Organisation must exist and pass vetting.
- **Alternative Outcomes:** Approved, Approved with capacity-building conditions, Rejected.
- **Escalation Conditions:** Partner is the only one with access to a besieged area, but failed a minor financial audit.
- **Review Triggers:** Annual MoU expiration.
- **Appeal Mechanisms:** Rejected NGOs can appeal to the regional HQ.
- **Human Override:** Executive waiver assuming full legal liability for a failed audit.
- **Uncertainty:** A partner's internal culture is hard to audit on paper.

## 2. Trust Suspension Decision
- **Purpose:** Immediately halting a partnership to mitigate severe risk.
- **Decision Maker:** Compliance Officer or Country Director.
- **Supporting Evidence:** Whistleblower reports (from Accountability domain), audit failures, media exposure.
- **Governing Policies:** Zero Tolerance policies for Safeguarding and Fraud.
- **Constraints:** Suspending a partner often immediately halts life-saving aid to beneficiaries.
- **Preconditions:** Credible allegation of severe breach.
- **Alternative Outcomes:** Suspended, Placed on strict monitoring, Cleared.
- **Escalation Conditions:** Allegation involves state intelligence or armed groups.
- **Review Triggers:** Conclusion of a formal external investigation.
- **Appeal Mechanisms:** The suspended organisation can present counter-evidence.
- **Human Override:** Rare; Zero Tolerance policies usually forbid overrides.
- **Uncertainty:** High risk of malicious, false whistleblower reports from rival NGOs.

## 3. Capability Accreditation Decision
- **Purpose:** Verifying that an organisation actually possesses the technical skills they claim.
- **Decision Maker:** Technical Sector Lead (e.g., Chief Medical Officer).
- **Supporting Evidence:** Past project reports, staff certifications, physical inspection of facilities.
- **Governing Policies:** Sphere Standards, WHO standards.
- **Constraints:** Lack of time to physically inspect remote field hospitals.
- **Preconditions:** Organisation claims a specific technical capability.
- **Alternative Outcomes:** Accredited, Denied, Conditionally Accredited (requires supervision).
- **Escalation Conditions:** State government revokes a medical license unilaterally.
- **Review Triggers:** High mortality rates reported in the partner's clinics.
- **Appeal Mechanisms:** Formal technical review panel.
- **Human Override:** Emergency acceptance of unverified capabilities during a mass casualty event.
- **Uncertainty:** A partner may have the equipment but lack trained staff at a specific moment.
