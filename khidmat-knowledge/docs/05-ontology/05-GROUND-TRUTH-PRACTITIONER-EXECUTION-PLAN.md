This document is the **execution plan** for testing the ontology against reality. It organizes the 47 `NOT YET REVIEWED` items from the Review Matrix into a practical interview structure for real humanitarian practitioners.

---

## A. Who Should Review? (Expertise Categories)

To adequately test the ontology, we require perspectives from practitioners who interact with different facets of humanitarian reality. The 47 review items map to three primary organizational vantage points:

1. **Field Operations & Casework (FO):** Practitioners engaged directly with affected populations (e.g., case managers, social workers, field officers, protection officers). They hold the ground truth on household structures, lived vulnerability, daily needs, and the realities of human persistence.
2. **MEAL & Information Management (IM):** Practitioners managing data, evidence, and outcomes (e.g., Monitoring, Evaluation, Accountability, and Learning officers, database managers). They hold the ground truth on missing information, conflicting claims, outcome tracking, and identity resolution.
3. **Programme Management & Coordination (PM):** Practitioners designing interventions and navigating the ecosystem (e.g., programme managers, inter-agency coordinators, donor liaisons). They hold the ground truth on rules, funding constraints, handoffs, and ecosystem boundaries.

## C. Cross-Perspective Review Items

The following items require testing with more than one expertise category to ensure the ontology bridges the gap between field reality and system reality:

- **Context-Dependency (GT-P2, GT-PL2):** Needs FO to describe how needs change on the ground, and PM to describe how programme rules shift across locations.
- **Identity Resolution (GT-P4, GT-OQ1, GT-AR6):** Needs FO to describe how they recognize people face-to-face, and IM to describe how system records are merged or separated.
- **Outcomes (GT-PL6, GT-OQ5):** Needs FO to describe when a case is considered "closed," and IM/PM to describe when a programme considers an outcome "achieved."
- **Rules and Conflicts (GT-P5, GT-L4):** Needs FO to describe rules clashing in a household visit, and PM to describe rules clashing at the donor level.

## D. Practitioner Review Groups & Question Sets

The questions below are designed to test reality without revealing the expected ontology answer, preventing confirmation bias.

### Group 1: Field Operations & Casework

*Focus: Human realities, conditions, relationships, and lived events.*

**1.1 Identity and Persistence (GT-P4, GT-OQ1/Q1, GT-L2, GT-AR3)**
- *Question:* How do you currently determine, without biometrics, that a new registration is the same person as an existing record? What goes wrong? Have you ever seen a person's life circumstances and their administrative status tracked in a way that caused confusion? Which entities (Person, Household, Family, Case) do you actually track as distinct things?

**1.2 Relationships and Household Structures (GT-P7, GT-L3, GT-OQ3/Q3, GT-OQ18/Q18)**
- *Question:* Describe a real case where someone's family and household didn't match (e.g., displacement, fostering). How did you decide who counted as part of which? How does a risk in one family member affect others who depend on them? Is there a meaningful difference in practice between an orphaned child and an unguardianed child?

**1.3 Conditions, Needs, and Vulnerability (GT-P1, GT-L1, GT-L5, GT-PL1, GT-PL3, GT-OQ2/Q2, GT-OQ4/Q4, GT-OQ11/Q11, GT-OQ16/Q16)**
- *Question:* What ongoing conditions do you track for a person or household (e.g., health, shelter, capability)? What specific values do they take? When assessing vulnerability, do you follow an explicit rule for how multiple factors combine, or is it a judgment call? Does grouping identity, health, and capability separate from household environment match how you think about a person? How do different needs interact (e.g., one blocking another)? What standard defines "enough" to determine if a need exists?

**1.4 Events and Case Timelines (GT-P6, GT-L6, GT-AR2)**
- *Question:* Walk through the specific dateable events in one real case from first contact to the most recent update. Are there moments better described as an ongoing state rather than an event? How often does a case genuinely reopen or get sent back to an earlier stage, versus proceeding straight through?

### Group 2: MEAL & Information Management

*Focus: Evidence, epistemology, contradiction, and outcomes.*

**2.1 Missing Information and Contradiction (GT-P3, GT-L7, GT-PL4, GT-OQ12/Q12, GT-OQ13/Q13, GT-AR6)**
- *Question:* How do you distinguish between "we checked and this is not true" and "we have not checked this yet" in your systems? When two sources disagree about the same fact, what actually happens to both pieces of information? When your system is unsure whether two records are the same person, what happens next?

**2.2 Evidence and Confidence (GT-OQ10/Q10, GT-AR4, GT-AR5)**
- *Question:* What kinds of evidence do you rely on (documents, testimony, observation), and do some carry more weight than others? What kinds of decisions always require human sign-off, regardless of automated confidence? Have you seen dignity or safeguarding concerns represented as a score/rating, or as a strict rule?

**2.3 Outcomes and Tracking (GT-PL6, GT-OQ5/Q5, GT-OQ9/Q9)**
- *Question:* Describe a case where "did the case close" and "did it actually work" were tracked differently, or by different people. Does treating "at risk" as an ongoing fact about a household match how you record data?

### Group 3: Programme Management & Coordination

*Focus: Contexts, rules, coordination, and ecosystem boundaries.*

**3.1 Context and Variation (GT-P2, GT-PL2, GT-AR1)**
- *Question:* When you say something is true of a situation, what determines if it would still be true somewhere else, or in another season? Can you give an example where the *same* household condition meant something different depending on the programme? Have you seen "needs assessment" or "coordination" mean genuinely different things at the case level versus the programme level?

**3.2 Constraints, Rules, and Consent (GT-P5, GT-L4, GT-OQ8/Q8, GT-OQ14/Q14)**
- *Question:* What rules bind your work regardless of the specific case? Describe a real situation where two rules bound your work in opposite directions (e.g., donor requirements vs. privacy) and how it was handled. What kinds of restrictions have you seen attached to funding? What do you actually ask consent for, and what happens if it is withdrawn partway through?

**3.3 Coordination and Ecosystem (GT-L8, GT-PL5, GT-PL7, GT-OQ6/Q6, GT-OQ7/Q7, GT-OQ15/Q15, GT-OQ17/Q17, GT-OQ19/Q19)**
- *Question:* Describe a real referral or handoff—what made it a handoff rather than a new case? Is there a meaningful difference between "an organisation" and "a programme" in practice? Do you interact with schools/clinics as parties with their own interests, or just as services? Is there a distinct layer of decision-making where funders themselves coordinate? Is there a distinct role in your work that exists solely to coordinate a case across organizations? When you describe assistance, do you describe *what need it addresses*, *how it's delivered*, and *why* separately or together? What does a donor relationship actually consist of?

## E. Review Method Instructions

1.  Use `05-GROUND-TRUTH-REVIEW-RECORD-TEMPLATE.md` to record responses.
2.  > A conversational interview question may cover multiple Review IDs, but practitioner evidence MUST be separated into individual review records when recorded. Each resulting record must identify exactly one Review ID.
    *Example:* `Interview Topic 1.1 → practitioner response → separate records for GT-P4, GT-OQ1, GT-L2, GT-AR3`.
    Disagreement must remain attributable to the individual Review ID, and the Matrix status must be updated per Review ID, never per interview topic.
3.  Do not force consensus. If practitioners disagree, record both perspectives.
4.  Practitioner responses are **evidence**. Translating that evidence into an ontology modification is a separate, subsequent governance step.
5.  Until genuine practitioner evidence is collected and recorded, all 47 items remain `NOT YET REVIEWED`.
