## 3. Primitive Sufficiency
**FACT FROM REPOSITORY:** The ontology relies on exactly seven primitives: Condition, Context, Epistemic Stance, Entity, Norm, Occurrence, Relation.
**ONTOLOGICAL ASSESSMENT:** These primitives are irreducible, universal, and mutually exclusive. They successfully categorize existence (Entity), bounding (Norm, Context), connection (Relation), temporal span (Condition), temporal point (Occurrence), and knowledge (Epistemic Stance). 
**INFERENCE:** No eighth primitive is required. The set is structurally complete.

## 4. Layer Sufficiency
**FACT FROM REPOSITORY:** Eight layers derive strictly from the primitives: Facets, Entities, Relationships, Constraints, States, Events, Cognition, Coordination Patterns.
**ONTOLOGICAL ASSESSMENT:** The layers perform genuine semantic roles rather than acting as database tables. The distinction between Facets (axes) and States (values) correctly acknowledges taxonomic gaps without breaking the structure. Coordination Patterns remain descriptive shapes, strictly separated from executable workflows.
**INFERENCE:** The layers are genuinely ontological and sufficient.

## 5. Pillar Sufficiency
**FACT FROM REPOSITORY:** Seven pillars slice vertically across the layers (Human & Social Subject, Context & Environment, Vulnerability & Need, Epistemics & Knowledge, Actors & Ecosystem, Action & Coordination, Resources & Support).
**ONTOLOGICAL ASSESSMENT:** The pillars act as valid domain views organizing the model without inventing new ontological categories. They successfully encompass the human subject independently from the administrative system, as well as the ecosystem of actors and resources.
**INFERENCE:** The pillars are sufficient for the domain scope and do not leak implementation concepts.

## 6. Core Semantic Boundaries
**FACT FROM REPOSITORY:** The ontology explicitly isolates Reality from Claim, Identifier from Sameness Claim, and Sameness Claim from Epistemic Stance. It also clearly separates Occurrence from State.
**ONTOLOGICAL ASSESSMENT:** These separations are critical to avoiding the common trap of equating "database record" with "human existence". Treating Identity as an Epistemic Stance regarding an Entity, grounded by Evidence, is semantically pristine and protects the open-world integrity of the system.
**INFERENCE:** The core boundaries are strictly necessary and highly defensible.

## 7. Temporal Sufficiency
**FACT FROM REPOSITORY:** Condition models spans of time; Occurrence models bounded points.
**ONTOLOGICAL ASSESSMENT:** The system can represent things that persist (Entity), happen (Occurrence), and change over time (Condition/State transitions). The refusal to collapse developmental/life trajectory and programme engagement (CCR-7) into a single clock is methodologically sound, and G2 correctly leaves it non-foreclosing pending localized evidence.
**INFERENCE:** Temporal representation is highly sufficient.

## 8. Humanitarian Reality Coverage
**FACT FROM REPOSITORY:** The ontology accommodates people, families, vulnerabilities, needs, risks, context, actors, interventions, outcomes, and cognition.
**ONTOLOGICAL ASSESSMENT:** It accurately represents reality independent of administrative representation. For example, a Need exists as a Condition independently of eligibility (Constraint). 
**INFERENCE:** All major humanitarian realities are representable without semantic distortion.

## 9. Open-World & Epistemic Sufficiency
**FACT FROM REPOSITORY:** The Cognition layer is derived from Epistemic Stance (P3). Absence of a statement is not its negation. Unknowns, contradictions, and uncertain confidence are explicitly modeled.
**ONTOLOGICAL ASSESSMENT:** This is the strongest feature of the ontology. By preserving "Unknown" and contradictory claims rather than overwriting them or assuming "False", the ontology actively prevents automated harm.
**INFERENCE:** The open-world commitment is coherent, usable, and sufficient.

## 10. Evidence Sufficiency
**FACT FROM REPOSITORY:** Evidence is an artifact (Entity/Occurrence) that grounds an Epistemic Stance. There is no dedicated Evidence primitive.
**ONTOLOGICAL ASSESSMENT:** This correctly positions evidence by its semantic role. Inventing a new primitive for Evidence would have been redundant, as evidence is merely a real-world object or event used to support a Claim.
**INFERENCE:** Evidence representation is sufficient; deep taxonomic detail is correctly deferred.

## 11. Relation Sufficiency
**FACT FROM REPOSITORY:** Relation connects entities that persist. 
**ONTOLOGICAL ASSESSMENT:** The Relation primitive adequately captures kinship, dependency, organizational hierarchies, and programmatic interactions. Need-interactions were evaluated, and the decision to treat them as Cognition/documentation rather than inventing new formal Relations (G4) prevents unnecessary complexity.
**INFERENCE:** The Relation primitive is highly sufficient.

## 12. Coordination Sufficiency
**FACT FROM REPOSITORY:** Coordination Patterns represent recurring shapes of interaction across multiple parties.
**ONTOLOGICAL ASSESSMENT:** The ontology actively polices the boundary between Coordination Patterns and workflows (LCR-7). It refuses to dictate queues, routing, or state machines.
**INFERENCE:** Coordination is handled properly at the ontology level without descending into orchestration.

## 13. Implementation Contamination Check
**FACT FROM REPOSITORY:** Architecture rules (CCR-2, LCR-7) quarantine workflow execution. Database keys, APIs, and microservices do not appear in the foundational layers.
**ONTOLOGICAL ASSESSMENT:** The ontology rigorously defends its conceptual purity. It acts as a semantic constraint system and avoids dictating software architecture.
**INFERENCE:** The ontology is entirely free from implementation contamination.

## 14. Cross-Document Consistency
**FACT FROM REPOSITORY:** Stage 4 and Stage 7 resolved prior conflicts (e.g., Need/Risk as Condition, Organisation/Programme split).
**ONTOLOGICAL ASSESSMENT:** The authoritative documents are synchronized. The primitive definitions, layer structures, and architectural rules are consistent across the board.
**INFERENCE:** Cross-document consistency is exceptionally high.

**1. Are the 7 primitives sufficient?**
YES. They completely classify existence, states, bounding, connections, and epistemology without semantic gaps.

NO. Strict boundaries prevent overloading (e.g., Risk is a Condition, the confidence about it is an Epistemic Stance).

NO. They are mutually exclusive. Span (Condition) cannot substitute for point (Occurrence).

YES. They organize the primitives meaningfully into domain realities (Entities, States, Constraints) without prescribing software tables.

YES. They slice the layers logically to represent full humanitarian domains (e.g., Context & Environment, Actors & Ecosystem).

YES. The ontology definitively splits the existence of a condition (Reality) from the assertion of it (Claim), the artifact proving it (Evidence), and the system's confidence in it (Epistemic Stance).

YES. Identity is not reduced to a database string; it is properly modeled as an Epistemic Stance about an Entity.

**8. Is Sameness Claim distinct from Epistemic Stance?**
YES. The claim asserts sameness; the Epistemic Stance holds the warrant/confidence in that claim.

**9. Is Occurrence vs State semantically defensible?**
YES. The distinction between a happening (Occurrence) and an ongoing truth (State) is essential to differentiating actions (delivery) from impact (wellbeing).

NO. Only specific taxonomies and implementation details are missing, which is correct for this phase.

**18. Is implementation thinking contaminating the ontology?**
NO. It fiercely quarantines workflows, automation, and database constructs.

**19. Can downstream architecture proceed without inventing foundational ontology concepts?**
YES. The foundation provides all necessary structural categories.
