1 Purpose

What business domain is being described, and what this document is not.

This document describes humanitarian reality itself — the people, circumstances, needs and decisions that constitute humanitarian work — independently of any software, organisation or technology that operates on it.

This document isThis document is not
A description of the domain being modelledA system specification or architecture
The business reference for all downstream designA data model, schema or database design
Organised around reality — people, needs, riskOrganised around software modules or workflows
Explicit about what is known and unknownA statement that all questions are answered
Governing distinction

Reality is separated from software throughout. A malnourished child is malnourished whether or not any system records it. Registration, verification and delivery are things organisations do; they are not what exists.

2 Vision

Why Khidmat exists.

Humanitarian systems store data but rarely understand it. Most humanitarian software registers cases and tracks donations rather than understanding the people and circumstances behind them.

Four consequences of that gap

Duplicate registration

The same person is not recognised across engagements.

Inconsistent verification

Quality varies with who performed it.

Lost history

A person is captured as a point in time, not a trajectory.

Unmeasured outcomes

Assistance is recorded; improvement is not.

The six questions the domain must answer

Most systems answer what did the person ask for? Khidmat must answer:

  1. What does this person need?
  2. Why do they need it?
  3. What will happen if the need is unmet?
  4. Who else is affected?
  5. What support pathway exists?
  6. What future risks are developing?
Definition of success

Success is not measured by aid distributed or cases closed. Success is sustainable improvement in human wellbeing, and reduced dependency over time.

3 Humanitarian Operating Model

Three structural properties of how humanitarian work actually operates.

3.1 Two altitudes running concurrently

Humanitarian work runs two structurally distinct rhythms at once. This is the strongest evidenced finding in the domain research, corroborated independently across five studies of sector practice.

AltitudeCadenceGoverns
ProgrammeAnnual or crisis-lengthWhat capacity, funding and eligibility exist at all
CaseEvent-drivenHow one person's situation unfolds within that capacity
Consequence

The same words mean different things at each altitude. Needs assessment, planning, monitoring and coordination all exist at both and describe genuinely different activities. Any term used across both must state which altitude it means.

3.2 Two flows, structurally decoupled

Knowledge flow

Registration, evidence, verification, assessment — building understanding of a situation.

Material flow

Procurement, logistics, delivery — moving resources to people.

Sector practice separates these. Khidmat is positioned in the knowledge flow: it builds the understanding that the material flow acts upon.

3.3 Understanding precedes action

The operating sequence is Reality → Knowledge → Understanding → Reasoning → Responsible action. Consequential decisions — eligibility, escalation, exit — remain human decisions with a human accountable for them.

Two points worth the Lead's attention

Organisation and Programme are separate. Every external source examined names implementing organisations separately from the programmes they lead. Collapsing them loses the distinction between who acts and what funds the action.

The five operational roles above are unverified. Registrant, proxy, field verifier, human reviewer and case manager are supported by nothing outside this project. Sector standards operate at organisational level and do not describe roles at this granularity.

6 Humanitarian Reality

What exists, organised by reality rather than by process.

6.1 The person

DimensionContent
PersistenceA person is a persistent entity, not a per-case record. The same human recognised across registrations, cases and years.
IdentityName, age, gender, marital status, documentation status
Life stageNine developmental stages — each a distinct reality carrying its own dependencies and vulnerabilities, not merely an age band
CapabilityPhysical · cognitive · educational · economic · caregiving
HealthAcute · chronic · disability · mental health · nutritional
WellbeingThe state the whole system exists to improve
DignityNot a measurable condition — a standing constraint on how everything else may be used

6.2 Family, household and community

A family is not a list of people; a household is a living unit; they are not the same thing and routinely diverge.

Family

Relationships, dependency, and who is responsible for income, care, education and decisions.

Household

Housing, utilities, shelter condition, and resilience — the capacity to absorb a shock.

Community

Settlement, services and the real distance and quality of access to them, local assets, social capital.

Why isolation fails

Vulnerability cascades along dependency. A mother's risk is her infant's risk. A person cannot be assessed apart from those who depend on them.

6.3 Environment

Geography, settlement type, infrastructure, markets and seasonality. Environment changes what a condition means: the seasonal calendar turns a damaged roof in a flood zone before monsoon into a preventive emergency rather than a routine repair. Severity is never a property of a condition alone.

6.4 Situations, needs, risk and strengths

ConceptDefinitionKey property
SituationA circumstance that changes wellbeing — illness, widowhood, disability, job loss, violence, displacement, education interruptionSituations are not needs; they are what needs arise from
NeedA gap between current state and a basic standard of wellbeingDynamic — opens, changes severity, resolves or expires
VulnerabilityEmerges from multiple compounding factors, not one conditionComposition rule undefined
RiskCurrent, future, compound and long-term, each with horizon, trend and severityProduces signals; does not decide
Capability & strengthWhat a person, household and community can do; assets and opportunityDependency reduction is impossible if only deficits are modelled
OutcomeImproved, held, or deterioratedNegative outcomes must be representable
Seven need categories

Food · Health · Education · Housing · Livelihood · Psychosocial · Protection. Whether this set is closed, or must accommodate a situation nobody anticipated, is an open question for the Lead.

6.5 Evidence and what is known

Humanitarian reality includes not only how things are, but how well anyone knows it. Two organisations looking at the same household hold genuinely different states of knowledge about it.

→ClaimWhat someone states. Carries quality: how complete, how internally consistent. Who reported it changes its weight.
→EvidenceWhat grounds the claim.
→FindingWhat verification produced from the claim.
→Assessed needCarries an explicit confidence, openly held.

8 End-to-End Humanitarian Flow

How understanding is built and acted upon.

01Need identifiedRequested, implied, or observed
02RegistrationProduces claims, not facts
03Evidence collectionPer the case and the need
04VerificationClaims become findings
05AssessmentNeeds identified, with confidence
06Decision supportRecommendation with stated uncertainty
07Eligibility & approvalA human decision, gated by an approved plan — never an automatic status flip
08Support planningSector, modality and phase selected
09DeliverySupport reaches the person
10Monitoring & follow-upMay reopen any earlier stage
11Outcome measurementImproved, held, or deteriorated
12Recovery & resilienceCapacity to absorb the next shock
Not a straight line

Reopening is standard professional practice, not exception handling. Monitoring routinely returns a case to reassessment; a grievance, a changed circumstance or new information can send it back to any earlier stage.

9 Beneficiary Journey

The evolution of a person's situation over time — which continues whether or not anyone is acting on it.

→Human existsA person, in a family, in a household, in a community
→Situation changesIllness, loss, displacement, disaster, a season turning
→Need emergesA gap opens against a basic standard of wellbeing
→Need communicatedRequested, implied, observed — or not communicated at all
→Understanding builtClaims gathered, context assembled, verified, assessed
→Support planned and deliveredA human decides; support reaches the person
→OutcomeWellbeing improved, held, or deteriorated
→Recovery & resilienceThe generating situation resolves; capacity to absorb the next shock returns
→Long-term wellbeingSustained independence — or continued dependency

Four properties

It is the person's

Not the organisation's. It continues without any humanitarian actor knowing.

It is not a single position

A household can be recovering on one need and in crisis on another.

It is not linear

People revisit earlier stages; recovery does not move in a straight line.

It can end without recovery

Nothing guarantees needs are met. A model assuming the endpoint misdescribes every case that never reaches it.

Never conflate two clocks

Where a person is in their life is not where they are in your process. Mature sector practice tracks these as two separate concepts, never one combined status field.

10 Giving Journey

The resourcing side of the domain — and the largest known gap in this reference.

Status — not described

The giving side is in scope for the domain and has no domain description. No authoritative source in this project describes how giving works, and the concept of a giver adopting a family or case appears in no source at all.

What follows is therefore presented as the intended shape, unvalidated — not as a described part of the domain. It is included so the gap is visible rather than silently absent.

10.1 What is established

StatementBasis
Givers are recognised humanitarian actorsSector standards explicitly define humanitarian actors to include those providing financial, material or technical support without directly delivering assistance
Giving operates in formal and informal formsDocumented distinction between institutional and community-level philanthropy
Funding restrictions determine who may receive whatRecognised as real; the content of such restrictions is not described anywhere
Donor obligations and beneficiary accountability structurally conflictWell-documented sector tension across multiple independent studies

10.2 Intended shape — unvalidated

→Verified need existsEstablished — output of the beneficiary journey
→Need becomes visible to giversUnvalidated — disclosure rules undescribed
→Giver commits supportUnvalidated — one-time or sustained
→Adoption of a family or caseNo source — exclusivity, duration, consent and termination all undefined
→Delivery and re-verificationEstablished on the beneficiary side
What is needed to close this

A working session describing giving at the same depth the human side is already described: who gives, on what terms, what obligations travel with a resource, what adoption actually is, and how restricted funding constrains eligibility. This is knowledge the business holds and no document in this project contains.

11 AI Operating Model

How intelligence is expected to behave in this domain.

Augments judgment

Supports human decisions. Does not replace them.

Reasons from what is known

Reactive inference from stated and observed information — not prediction before any signal exists.

States its confidence

Every important conclusion carries how well it is known and what would change it.

Defers when consequence is high

Eligibility, escalation and exit remain human decisions with a human accountable.

The single most important behaviour

Absence of information is not a negative finding. "We have no record that this household was displaced" is a different statement from "this household was not displaced." A system that cannot say I do not know — distinctly from no — will be confidently wrong, and that confusion is the largest single source of unreliable automated behaviour.

This is why the system must model not only the world, but what it is entitled to believe about the world.

12 Domain Boundaries

Four distinct circles that are routinely and damagingly confused.

Humanitarian realityEverything that exists — including things Khidmat will never build. Givers, government responsibility, informal mutual aid, programme coordination, crisis phase.
Domain scopeWhat Khidmat must understand to help people correctly. Currently: all of the reality described in this document.
First build scopeWhat is delivered first. Narrower — see §13.
RoadmapReal, described, and built later — see §14.
Governing rule

Being real does not mean being in scope, and being out of scope does not mean being unreal. Givers are humanitarian actors and are not built first. Both statements are true; neither cancels the other. Collapsing them is how a project either builds what it excluded, or denies what exists.

A boundary requiring deliberate decision

Biometric identity

Facial recognition is currently out of the first build. It would solve a real and open problem — recognising the same person across engagements. But it targets vulnerable people including children and survivors of violence, and established sector data-protection guidance governs it. If it enters scope it should do so through an explicit decision consulting that guidance, not silently because it is convenient.

13 Current Scope

What the first build understands.

  • Person, family, household and community, including household resilience
  • Life stage, capability and health dimensions
  • Needs, and confidence-weighted needs assessment
  • Vulnerability and risk, with horizon, trend and severity
  • Community and environmental context, including seasonality
  • Evidence, verification, and the claim-to-finding transition
  • Case orchestration and engagement state
  • Claim quality, confidence, and temporal reasoning
Two gaps inside current scope

Consent is a foundational principle with a placeholder mechanism. Grievance handling is a foundational principle with no mechanism — while sector practice treats it as a standing capability maintained by every major humanitarian organisation. Both sit oddly against the commitments they are meant to implement.

TermMeaning in this domain

EvidenceWhat grounds a claim in fact.
FindingWhat verification produces from a claim.
Household resilienceCapacity to absorb, adapt to and recover from a shock. Two households with identical needs are not equally vulnerable.

NeedA gap between current state and a basic standard of wellbeing.
