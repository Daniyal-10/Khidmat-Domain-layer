1 Domain Primitives

The irreducible categories of concept that everything else in the ontology classifies under.

A Domain Primitive is not a humanitarian concept in its own right — it is the answer to the question "what kind of thing is this?", asked of every concept in the domain until the answers stop multiplying and begin repeating. Seven such categories were reached, each grounded in the Humanitarian Domain Reference Model and tested against the domain's least-described area (the giving side) to confirm the set does not need to grow as new content arrives.

2 Ontology Layers

Eight structural layers, each derived from a named primitive, that hold the actual content of the domain.

Layers are where domain concepts are structured for use — dimensions, persisting things, connections, rules, values over time, dateable happenings, warrant, and recurring multi-party shapes. Every layer traces to one or more of the seven primitives; nothing appears in a layer without a named source.

All seven primitives are consumed across the eight layers, and no layer introduces a category absent from the primitive set. Condition and Context each feed more than one layer by design — Condition separates the dimension something varies along (Facets) from the value it holds at a given time (States), which makes visible that the domain currently has many dimensions and comparatively few populated values.

Where the layers are strongest and weakest

Layers describing how organisations operate — Constraints, Events, Coordination Patterns — are the best evidenced. Layers describing people directly — States, social Relationships, human Facets — were historically the thinnest, until ground-truth practitioner review (§5) substantially strengthened them.

3 Ontology Pillars

Seven vertical domain views across the layers — not an additional structural category.

A pillar groups entities, relationships, states, events, and cognitive content into a coherent area of humanitarian reality. Pillars never introduce a primitive or a layer of their own; citation always runs primitives → layers → pillars, never the reverse.

I · Human & Social Subject

The person, and the family/household fabric around them

II · Context & Environment

The physical, economic, and temporal frame that changes what a condition means

III · Vulnerability & Need

The open-ended situations, needs, and compounding vulnerability that drive response

IV · Epistemics & Knowledge

What is known, how well, and what is explicitly not known

V · Actors & Ecosystem

Organisations, programmes, donors, government, and community actors

VI · Action & Coordination

How help happens over time, and how actors hand work to one another

VII · Resources & Support

The sector, modality, and phase dimensions of assistance

A full-domain coverage test confirmed the seven pillars jointly cover the Reference Model's content without leaving a concept unmapped and without forcing any workflow concept into pillar status.

4 Architecture Rules

The grammar the ontology must obey — not the software architecture itself.

Architecture Rules govern how the primitives, layers, and pillars may be composed, extended, or corrected. They are deliberately kept apart from implementation: ontology rules ≠ database schema ≠ API design ≠ workflow engine ≠ AI-agent architecture. No rule below specifies a table, an endpoint, a queue, or an agent behaviour.

Primitive closure

The seven-primitive set is treated as complete; extension requires escalation, not accommodation

One primary classification

Every concept gets exactly one primary primitive; apparent dual needs are escalated as a tension

Reality / scope separation

Being real does not imply being in scope; being out of scope does not imply being unreal

Context frames, never participates

A frame that is itself tracked and funded has become an Entity, not a Context

Norm/Context pairing

A rule binding in one scope and not another is one Constraint plus one Context, never two Constraints

Occurrence vs. State

An Occurrence is complete once it has happened; anything still true afterward is the State it produced

Cognition non-assertion

Cognition never asserts a fact about the world — it records the warrant for what other layers assert

Open-world commitment

Absence of a recorded statement is never the negation of that statement

Human-oversight trigger

Decisions materially affecting rights, safety, dignity, or eligibility route to human review

Non-linearity

Every multi-stage pattern supports re-entry to an earlier stage as ordinary, not exceptional

Dignity as constraint

Dignity is represented as a standing rule on how content may be used — never as a score

Outcome/Impact vs. closure

Whether a case is closed and whether it worked are kept as distinct representations

Implementation quarantine

Registration, verification, assessment, delivery, and similar actions appear in the ontology because understanding is acquired through them — but they may never become the ontology's organising axis. Modelling around activity produces a record of the organisation, not a model of the people it serves.

5 Ground Truth Reviews

The foundation was tested against real practitioner accounts, not left as a theoretical construction.

A structured review matrix covering every primitive, layer, pillar, and domain-bearing architecture rule — plus every previously named open question — was put to practitioners across field operations, MEAL/information management, and programme coordination. Each response was recorded against a fixed vocabulary (Confirmed, Challenged, Refined, Missing, Context-dependent, Unresolved, Not assessable) rather than folded into a predetermined narrative.

The two challenged items both concerned whether Organisation and Programme should be treated as one actor or two — practitioners consistently described them as distinct, which was carried into governance (§7). The one unresolved item concerned the dual-clock separation of a person's life trajectory from their administrative status, where the evidence offered was compatible with, but did not conclusively establish, the proposed rule.

The result of this stage is not primarily the count. It is that the foundation was deliberately exposed to disconfirmation, and where it was found wanting, that finding was carried forward rather than smoothed over.

6 Evidence Integration

Ground-truth findings were weighed, not automatically promoted into universal ontology structure.

Every review outcome was mapped against the primitive, layer, pillar, or architecture rule it bore on, and the strength of that evidence was explicitly tracked alongside the resulting change. A single practitioner's account, however clear, was treated differently from a finding corroborated across several independent reviews.

Carried forward with confidence

  • Facets, Entities, Relationships, Constraints, Events, and Cognition structures — all confirmed as designed
  • Compound vulnerability as an emergent, context-shaped condition rather than a fixed formula
  • The open-world commitment: unknown information preserved as unknown, not treated as false
  • Non-linearity and altitude qualification, both independently confirmed by practitioners

Carried forward as governed provisional

  • Outcome/Impact ownership (case journey vs. a separate MEAL function)
  • Service Providers as active, decision-making Entities
  • Funder-level coordination as a distinct pattern
  • Case Coordination/Orchestration as a distinct function
  • Need-to-Need interactions, deliberately kept out of formal Relation structure

Each governed-provisional item rests on a single practitioner account and is recorded as such — a workable basis for current modelling, not an empirically settled universal claim. Reopening any of these requires new corroborating evidence or an explicit superseding governance decision, not silent drift in a later document.

What governance does not do

A governed-provisional ruling is a modelling accommodation, not proof that the underlying domain proposition is empirically settled. The dual-clock rule, in particular, is preserved exactly as unresolved, non-mandatory, and non-foreclosing — it constrains nothing until further evidence or an explicit new ruling changes that.

8 Key Semantic Boundaries

The distinctions that keep the ontology from silently conflating different kinds of thing.

Reality / knowledge boundary

Reality ≠ Claim ≠ Evidence ≠ Epistemic Stance

What exists, what is asserted about it, what grounds the assertion, and how much warrant is held for it are four different things, never merged into one field.

Identity boundary

Entity → Identifier / Reference → Sameness Claim → Epistemic Stance

A person is the Entity. An identifier (phone number, national ID, internal ID) is a means of referring to that Entity, not the Entity itself. Asserting that two references denote the same person is a Claim. How confidently that claim is held is the Epistemic Stance. None of these four collapses into another.

Temporal boundary

Occurrence ≠ State

An Occurrence is something that happens, possibly over a bounded period (a verification visit, a displacement journey). A State is a condition that holds and can be re-examined later. An Occurrence may establish, change, or end a State — it is never the State itself.

Need / Risk

Need ≠ Risk

Both are classified as Conditions, but a Need is a present gap against a wellbeing standard, while Risk is oriented toward something that has not yet happened, carrying its own horizon, trend, and severity.

Organisation / Programme

Organisation ≠ Programme

An operator and the specific intervention it runs are distinct Entities. Programme-specific eligibility, funding, and activity rules attach to the Programme, not to the Organisation that operates it.

Outcome / Impact / Closure

Outcome / Impact ≠ Case Closure

Whether a case is administratively closed and whether the assistance actually worked are tracked separately, potentially by different people on different timelines.

Open-world boundary

Unknown ≠ False

Absence of a recorded statement is never treated as its negation. "No record of displacement" and "confirmed not displaced" must remain distinguishable.

9 Humanitarian Reality Coverage

Sufficient coverage for the current intended Khidmat scope — not a claim of exhaustive humanitarian coverage.

Across the seven pillars, the ontology represents: the person, family, and household, with the social relationships that connect them; context and environment, including seasonality and settlement; vulnerability, need, and risk, including their compounding and dynamic character; a basic-wellbeing standard as a norm; organisations, programmes, donors, government bodies, service providers, and emergent community actors; claims, evidence, epistemic confidence, uncertainty, and contradiction; states and dateable events; outcomes and impact as conditions distinct from their measurement; constraints including consent, safeguarding, and funding restrictions; and coordination patterns such as handoffs, referrals, reassessment loops, and grievance channels.

Scope statement

This is sufficient coverage for the current intended Khidmat scope, verified against the domain reference model and practitioner ground truth. It is not a claim that every possible humanitarian reality, in every context, is represented.

10 What Was Deliberately Not Added

Minimum sufficiency does not require modelling every future detail — it requires knowing which details are safely deferred.

Deferred / non-foundational

  • The precise identity-resolution matching mechanism
  • A vulnerability or compound-risk composition formula
  • A full evidence-kind taxonomy and relative epistemic weighting
  • Human-facet value sets (health, capability, lifecycle categories)
  • Detailed giving-side entities and patterns
  • A complete funding-restriction taxonomy
  • Whether the set of humanitarian situations is open or closed
  • Broader corroboration of governed-provisional items (§6–7)
  • Resolution of the dual-clock rule (CCR-7)

Foundational — closed / verified

  • Seven primitives, closed and coverage-tested
  • Eight layers, each with a named primitive source
  • Seven pillars, functioning as vertical views only
  • Architecture rules governing composition and extension
  • Organisation/Programme, Risk, and Need classifications
  • The open-world and human-oversight commitments

Implementation concerns — database schema, API contracts, a concrete representation of epistemic status, workflow routing, deduplication algorithms, and AI-agent architecture — sit downstream of this document entirely and are not addressed here.

12 What "Complete" Means

Complete does not mean exhaustive, and it does not mean immutable forever.

It means that the foundational ontology required for the current project scope has been established, tested, evidenced, governed, and verified as sufficient for downstream work — without requiring the invention of an additional foundational category, layer, or pillar. Future evolution remains possible if new evidence, or a genuinely expanded scope, demonstrates an actual foundational gap. That is not the same as expecting or requiring further foundational change now.
