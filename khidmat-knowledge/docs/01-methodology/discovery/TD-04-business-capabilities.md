# TD-04 — Business Capabilities

## 1. Scope

In scope: naming and evidencing the recurring things a humanitarian organization must be able to do (BMP Blueprint §6, Chapter 4's own framing), independent of who performs them or what future domain list Khidmat eventually builds — per Chapter 4's explicit litmus test, "written independently of any future domain list, so that list can later be validated against it rather than the other way around." Out of scope, unchanged from every prior dossier: BMP prose, HBRM entries, Business Architecture, Domain Discovery, ontology, taxonomy, schemas, or any implementation artifact. External sources below are evidence for testing claims about actual operating practice, not the deliverable — consistent with this session's explicit instruction and the practice already established in TD-03.

## 2. Research Questions

Derived from BMP Blueprint §7's Chapter 4 knowledge-required items:

- **Q1 (Capability catalogue):** What are the recurring things a humanitarian organization must be able to do, named and evidenced independently of Khidmat's own existing internal domain list?
- **Q2 (Completeness check):** Is Khidmat's own existing internal capability framing (`PROJECT_OVERVIEW.md` Ch6.1) a complete rendering of humanitarian business capabilities, or does it omit capabilities that Khidmat's own later, more detailed planning documents already presume exist — and might a future domain list contain a capability with no clear humanitarian-business justification? (BMP Blueprint §7 raises both directions of this risk explicitly.)

**Why this topic is next:** BMP Blueprint §6 states Chapter 4 depends on Chapter 3, handed off in TD-03. TD-03's own AR-006 (whether outcome/impact/learning belong inside the case lifecycle or as a separate capability) was left explicitly open and is a direct, load-bearing input to this topic — Q2 is written to pick that question up rather than re-derive it.

## 3. Tier C — Internal Discovery

**Observations** (direct textual facts, not yet inference):

- **TC4-1.** `PROJECT_OVERVIEW.md` Ch6.1 names a "Core Cognitive Lifecycle" of four sequential capabilities: *Evidence & Knowledge Acquisition, Understanding Formation, Reasoning & Justified Recommendation, Responsible Action.*
- **TC4-2.** The same section names three "Cross-Cutting Capabilities," explicitly distinguished from the sequential four as *"capabilities that condition how the core lifecycle operates over time and across organizations, rather than sequential stages within it"*: *Verification, Continuity & Re-assessment, Cross-Organizational Coordination.*
- **TC4-3.** The same section names two "Emergent Properties" — *Trust, Shared Humanitarian Understanding* — explicitly stated to be *"not capabilities that any organization performs, but outcomes that result from performing the above well over time."* The document itself already distinguishes a performed capability from a resulting outcome; this discipline is inherited, not invented, by this dossier.
- **TC4-4.** `KHIDMAT_BUSINESS_LOGIC_BLUEPRINT_V1.md` §16 lists domains already delivered (Human Model, Family/Household, Community Context, Needs + Needs Assessment, Vulnerability/Risk, Verification Operations, Case Management orchestration, Beneficiary Lifecycle, Claim epistemics) and domains planned but not yet delivered (Support intervention taxonomy, Support Delivery, **Outcome-indicator vocabulary; Impact measurement**, Programs, Volunteer Operations, Consent & Privacy, Persistent Person entity, **Beneficiary feedback / grievance handling**).

**Inference drawn from these observations (labeled as inference, not a Finding):** comparing TC4-1/2/3's seven named capabilities against TC4-4's domain list, at least three domain-list items do not map cleanly onto any of the seven: *Case Management orchestration* (delivered), *Outcome-indicator vocabulary/Impact measurement* (planned), and *Beneficiary feedback/grievance handling* (planned). This is an internal-document cross-check, not yet externally tested — Tier B/D collection below targets exactly these three gaps.

## 7. Findings

**BD-TD04-001 — Monitoring, Evaluation, Accountability, and Learning (MEAL) is a distinct, sector-standard capability bundle, not a tail-end stage of case management.**

*Confidence: High.* Corroboration: ≥4 independent institutional source families (a humanitarian professional network, a major INGO, a donor-community resource, a humanitarian training provider).

- **What this tells us about actual operating practice:** Organizations run monitoring, evaluation, accountability, and learning as a named, bundled, semi-independent capability — often a dedicated function or team — operating on its own cadence across whatever case-level or programme-level work it observes, not as the final stage of any one case's journey.
- **Why this matters for understanding the humanitarian business:** This directly and substantially informs TD-03's still-open AR-006 (whether outcome/impact/learning belong inside the case lifecycle, as `KHIDMAT_BUSINESS_LOGIC_BLUEPRINT_V1.md` §14 currently structures them, or as a separate capability). This Finding does not itself resolve AR-006 — that remains an open, explicitly-flagged assumption for a future BMP author — but it substantially strengthens the evidence on one side of that question with a concrete, named, real-world capability.
- **Universal, common, or context-specific:** Common/near-universal — MEAL is standard vocabulary across the INGO sources reviewed, not an isolated practice.
- **Reinforces, refines, or challenges previous discovery:** **Reinforces and sharpens** TD-03's BD-TD03-003 (sector practice generally separates case closure from outcome measurement) by supplying the specific, named capability that occupies that separated space.

---

**BD-TD04-002 — Complaints and Feedback Mechanisms (CFM) are a distinct, named, standard accountability capability, structurally separate from case management.**

*Confidence: High.* Corroboration: 5 independent institutional sources (IOM, UNHCR, CARE, DRC, and referenced NRC guidance), each maintaining dedicated CFM doctrine.

- **What this tells us about actual operating practice:** Organizations run a dedicated, standing channel for affected people to raise complaints and feedback, structurally separate from case management, with explicit referral pathways connecting the two ("referral pathways flexibly link CFM users requiring specialized services such as case management") — two different systems that hand off to each other, not one system.
- **Why this matters for understanding the humanitarian business:** Directly corroborates and sharpens TD-02's TC2-4 (accountability runs in both directions) and BD-TD02-001 (donor vs. affected-population accountability tension) by supplying the concrete, named capability that operationalizes affected-population accountability. It also directly corresponds to `KHIDMAT_BUSINESS_LOGIC_BLUEPRINT_V1.md` §16's own planned-but-undelivered "Beneficiary feedback / grievance handling" item — external evidence shows this is a named, standard, widely-documented capability across the sector's major actors, not a minor or optional feature.
- **Universal, common, or context-specific:** Common/standard — corroborated across 5 independent major humanitarian actors' own institutional guidance.
- **Reinforces, refines, or challenges previous discovery:** **Reinforces** TD-02's accountability findings with a concrete capability, and **refines** the internal domain list (`KHIDMAT_BUSINESS_LOGIC_BLUEPRINT_V1.md` §16) by confirming grievance handling merits its own capability status rather than being a minor feature of case management.

---

**BD-TD04-003 — Sector-recognized "core" capabilities (needs assessment, planning, coordination, monitoring) are consistently described at the programme/coordination level, distinct from and prerequisite to individual case-level capabilities of the same or similar name.**

*Confidence: High.* Corroboration: 4 independent sources (OCHA oPt, fscluster.org handbook, plus the UNHCR/IASC material already reviewed in TD-03), extending TD-03's BD-TD03-001.

- **What this tells us about actual operating practice:** "Needs assessment," "coordination," and "planning" exist as named capabilities at the sector/programme level (producing a joint, harmonized situation analysis before or alongside individual case work) — a different activity from a single case's own individual needs assessment, even though the two share a name.
- **Why this matters for understanding the humanitarian business:** A future capability catalogue cannot name "Needs Assessment" once without specifying which altitude it operates at, or it will silently conflate two genuinely different activities that happen to share a label. This is a concrete, capability-level instance of the altitude-split pattern the phase-1 review already flagged as recurring (TD-01's actor coordination-vs-operational split; TD-03's programme-cycle-vs-case-cycle split) — now observed a third, independent time.
- **Universal, common, or context-specific:** Common/near-universal within UN-coordinated response — same scope caveat already stated for TD-03's BD-TD03-001, not re-argued here.
- **Reinforces, refines, or challenges previous discovery:** **Reinforces** the altitude-split pattern with a third independent occurrence.

---

**BD-TD04-004 — Khidmat's own foundational capability framing (`PROJECT_OVERVIEW.md` Ch6.1) does not yet account for at least two sector-standard, now externally-evidenced capabilities (MEAL, CFM), and a third candidate gap (case coordination/orchestration) remains internally inferred but not yet independently tested.**

*Confidence: Medium-High for the two externally-corroborated gaps; Medium for the third.* Source: Tier C internal comparison (§3 above), now partly corroborated by BD-TD04-001 and BD-TD04-002.

- **What this tells us about actual operating practice:** Not directly about the sector — this Finding is about the current state of Khidmat's own internal capability description relative to both sector practice and Khidmat's own later planning work.
- **Why this matters for understanding the humanitarian business:** This gives a future BMP Chapter 4 author a concrete, evidenced starting list of candidate additions rather than a blank page: MEAL (evidenced), Complaints & Feedback/Accountability-to-affected-people (evidenced), and Case Coordination/Orchestration (not yet independently evidenced — an open gap, not a finding).
- **Universal, common, or context-specific:** The two evidenced gaps are common/standard sector practice; the third remains internal-only.
- **Reinforces, refines, or challenges previous discovery:** **Challenges** the completeness of `PROJECT_OVERVIEW.md` Ch6.1's capability list as currently stated — not its validity; the seven capabilities it names remain uncontradicted, only shown to be an incomplete rendering relative to newer evidence. Consistent with that document's own stated status ("before formal foundational documents are written"), this is recorded as a discovery finding for a future author to weigh, not a correction imposed on it.
